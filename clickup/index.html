The problem
"The format had won. Making a Story look professional was still hard."
Instagram's native tools covered the basics, and plenty of apps tried to fill the gap. But none gave a non-designer a reliable path to a polished, branded, magazine-quality Story — doing it well still took real design skill.

The opportunity
"The fastest-growing format in social, and we already knew the editing space."
Stories crossed 500M daily users. Inside Instasize it was already the second most-used format behind square. We knew the editing space and the people in it. The goal: professional results in seconds. No design skills required.

06 / 28

The constraints we designed within.

These weren't obstacles. They were the conditions that forced better decisions.
Sub-brand of Instasize
Building inside an existing ecosystem. Every naming and identity decision had to honor the parent brand while carving out distinct space.
Advanced design for non-designers
The audience had no design training and no patience for complexity. The tool had to make professional, polished results achievable in a few taps, hiding real sophistication behind a simple surface.
Freemium from day one
Monetization structure wasn't handed to us. Where you draw the line between free and paid is a product design problem, not a pricing decision.

What I learned
01
Curation requires conviction.
There is always pressure to add more, broaden the surface, accommodate every use case. Holding an editorial point of view under that pressure is a leadership skill, not just a design preference.
02
Freemium gating is a design problem.
Where you draw the line between free and paid changes the entire product experience. We iterated the gating logic multiple times before it felt right.
03
Leading designers is different from being one.
Your job becomes making the team effective, not producing the best individual work. That transition is harder than it looks from the outside.

What Knool is teaching me
01
Trust is a design problem.
AI capability was never the barrier. The barrier was giving experts a way to verify the output before they'd stake their professional reputation on it. Every trust feature we built came directly from attorney feedback.
02
Domain depth is a design advantage.
The reason the Prompt Library is organized by litigation phase and not by feature is because we spent months learning how attorneys think. That knowledge is embedded in the product. You can't copy it without doing the work.
